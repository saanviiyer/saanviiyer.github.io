Abstract

a protein benchmark reports the Spearman correlation between model scores and measured outcomes, which summarizes ranking quality over a whole assay. a design campaign does something else: it takes the top few candidates and tests them. those are different quantities, and this measures how much the first predicts the second, across 70 benchmark units in four corpora: 41 ProteinGym scans, 16 SSMuLA combinatorial libraries, three Rubisco kinetic axes from 8,760 single mutants, and 10 PETML plastic-degradation campaigns. reported correlation explains half the variance in top-10% selection value and 16% at top-1%, the same correlation delivers different selection value in different benchmark families, and a label-free skew diagnostic that looks promising reverses sign across corpora. where a supervised model has genuine signal the two improve together, so the claim is scoped, not blanket.

Finding 1

correlation becomes less informative exactly where campaigns act. it explains half the variance in top-10% selection value and 16% at top-1%. three of the 41 ProteinGym assays produce worse-than-random top-1% selections despite positive reported correlations, the worst at selection value −0.300 on a reported ρ of +0.105.

Finding 2

the same reported correlation means different things in different benchmark families. at matched correlation, SSMuLA libraries deliver +0.265 more top-1% selection value than ProteinGym assays (Mann-Whitney p = 0.0001), and +0.147 at top-10% (p = 0.0022). a second scorer family reproduces the offset at +0.252 (p = 0.0003). an aggregate leaderboard score mixes model behavior with benchmark composition, so two methods evaluated on different family mixtures are not comparable through the reported scalar alone.

Finding 3

the obvious label-free fix does not transfer. score-distribution skew looks promising retrospectively, correlating with residual top-1% selection value at +0.633 across 60 units and +0.489 within ProteinGym, but its sign reverses across families: fitted on ProteinGym and applied to SSMuLA it gives −0.388, and the reverse direction gives −0.582. a fourth corpus reverses it again, −0.783 within PETML against +0.551 within ProteinGym. skew adds information alongside the reported correlation, and fails as a standalone transferable rule.

Finding 4

the critique has a boundary, and reporting it is part of the result. where a supervised model has genuine signal, correlation and selection improve together: across 12 assays and three cross-validation schemes on identical folds, mean selection gain is +0.035 at both cutoffs against a bulk correlation gain of +0.031. a blanket claim that correlation is invalid would exceed the evidence. the narrower attribution problem survives: per-unit correlation and selection gains track at top-10% (ρ = +0.679) but not at top-1% (ρ = +0.168, p = 0.33), so an aggregate correlation gain does not identify which assays improved at a sharp cutoff.

what does the GEM-Bio side add?

a top-20% cutoff that all four corpora support, which is where the PETML result lives: selecting the top 20% of variants on 10 published plastic-degradation studies captures essentially none of the achievable gain, −0.016 for ESM-2 and −0.006 for ESM-C, against +0.326, +0.461 and +0.423 on SSMuLA, Rubisco and ProteinGym. and the Rubisco controls, the cleanest demonstration that a headline correlation is a composite: ESM-2 reaches ρ = +0.631 against maximum turnover across 8,760 single mutants, restricting to well-measured variants drops it to +0.364, and partialling out wild-type residue preference drops it to +0.259. positive signal survives, but the raw number was substantially measurement quality and site conservation.

what it adds up to

the number decays where it is used
reported correlation explains half the variance at top-10% and 16% at top-1%, the regime a campaign operates in.
the scalar is a composite
at matched correlation, benchmark family shifts selection value by +0.265, so a leaderboard scalar is not comparable across family mixtures.
the easy fix does not transfer
a label-free skew diagnostic looks useful in one corpus and flips sign in the next.
the claim is scoped
where a supervised model has real signal, correlation and selection rise together, so the critique names the regime rather than dismissing the metric.

Limitations. one scientific domain, and units within a corpus are not fully independent because they share experimental conventions. the family offset is estimated from ProteinGym and SSMuLA only; Rubisco and PETML show instability across regimes but are too few or too heterogeneous to estimate comparable offsets. the mechanism behind the offset is unidentified, and the between-corpus PETML comparison is underpowered at 10 studies.
