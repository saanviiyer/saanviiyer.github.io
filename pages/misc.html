Beyond Research

The habits I use in research carry into my music and side projects. I chase odd questions, and I learn the most when a quick test breaks.

Music

Music is the thing I keep coming back to outside the lab. I'm a singer and songwriter, and I produce my own tracks in GarageBand, layering vocals and instrumental parts until an idea becomes a finished song.

Most of my singing has been in choir. I sang lead alto in my school's top jazz choir and served as a section leader in both the jazz and women's choirs. I was selected three times as a Washington All-State and All-Northwest performer, singing in the Treble, Symphonic, and Mixed choirs.

Choir trained a part of me that research uses every day. I listen closely, and I try to make my small part serve the whole.

Selected side projects

These are small prototypes, usually built over a weekend to test one idea, not to become a company. They cluster around a few recurring interests.

Research tools

rxiver recommends and organizes arXiv papers, while Deadline Radar tracks ML, neuroscience, and computational-biology conference deadlines. Both came from friction in my own research workflow.

Human-in-the-loop automation

AuthzSwarm coordinates authorized defensive security checks. DraftDeck drafts social posts but never publishes them. Resume Tailor adapts application materials without inventing experience. Each one asks where an AI system should stop and hand control back to a person.

Creative computing

mixcut makes beat-aligned edits for DJ mixes, handsynth turns webcam hand-tracking into a browser instrument, and ConcertMatch connects listening habits to nearby live shows.

I have also experimented with goal-planning, job-matching, budgeting, and social-app prototypes. They were useful sketches. The projects above best represent the questions I keep returning to.
