what makes a predictive model build a usable internal variable, and what makes it keep one?

Abstract

most of my work asks whether a model's score means what it claims. here i ask the same question one level down, about the variable a model builds inside itself. a world model is a model that predicts what it will observe next, and to do that it has to hold an internal state. two questions follow. first, when does that state become a variable you can actually read, steer, and that the model's own dynamics use, rather than a tangle. i train action-conditioned world models on a robot's range sensors with no access to its heading, and a steerable heading variable appears only under partial observability. a blackout-trained model tracks true heading at a ring-coordinate fidelity of 0.99 while the best always-on control reaches only 0.61, so the two groups separate with no overlap, and the variable behaves like a continuous attractor that is steered causally. a companion study pushes the same question further, showing that a probe can read a coordinate out of the latent state that the dynamics never use, and that the coordinates the dynamics do use are set by what the observation makes separable. second, what happens to an old skill when a model keeps training. i compare biologically inspired learning rules against backpropagation at matched learning, and once the comparison is fair the retention advantage of locality is under a third of a point, while replay adds about ten. both halves are about the same thing. an internal representation is only as good as the pressure that shaped it.

01 · The Challenge

why an internal variable is the thing to measure

a world model is a model trained to predict its own next observation. to predict well when the world is partly hidden, it has to carry some internal state that stands in for what it cannot currently see, and the interesting question is what that state becomes. if it becomes a clean variable that lines up with something concrete, like which way the agent is facing, you can read it, trust it, and steer it. if it stays a tangle, the model can still predict while carrying nothing you can use. most work reads structure off a hidden state and calls it understanding, but structure is cheap. a ring can appear in a model that cannot actually track heading, so the shape alone proves nothing.

there is a second half to this. a variable a model has already built is not safe just because it exists, because the same training that teaches a new skill can quietly overwrite an old one. in a deployed model that is catastrophic forgetting with a cost attached, since the behavior being overwritten can be a capability or a safety property the model had before. both halves ask the same question from opposite ends. an internal representation is only as good as the pressure that shaped it and the pressure that is later allowed to reshape it. this matters because people increasingly read conclusions off a model's internal state and assume continued training only adds, and both assumptions are where this work pushes back.

02 · The Approach

two studies, one idea about internal state

both studies ask what shapes the variable a model carries, so i hold the measurement to one standard in the same way each time. in the heading study i never give the model pose and i never put a pose term in the loss, so any heading variable has to emerge on its own, and i measure fidelity only at the steps where the model must integrate, deep inside a blackout with no observation. in the continual-learning study i never let a fancy rule compete against a weak baseline, so i match the learning accuracy of every method before i compare how much it forgets. in both, the control is the point. a ring that appears in every model is not evidence until fidelity separates the conditions, and a retention gain is not evidence until the baseline is tuned as hard as the rule.

03 · Results

three findings

a steerable heading variable emerges only under partial observability

RoBoWoMo @ IEEE IROS 2026Lightning talk and poster

i train 36 action-conditioned world models on egocentric range observations from a robot moving in a plane, with no pose in the input and none in the loss, and ask whether a heading variable appears on its own. hidden states form a ring in nearly every condition, and in the recurrent backbones persistent homology finds one in 7 of 8 always-observed models and 7 of 8 shuffled-action controls that cannot integrate heading, so ring shape alone is not evidence. the deciding test is fidelity, how closely the coordinate around the ring follows physical heading at the steps where the model has to integrate without seeing anything. withholding observations during training lifts that fidelity by 0.421 over a matched shuffled-action null, with all 12 paired differences positive, and the lowest-fidelity blackout model, at 0.99, still beats the highest-fidelity always-on control, at 0.61, so the 36 models separate with no overlap. the steerable variable only appears when the task forces the model to hold it.

the steerable ring behaves like a continuous attractor, which is the structure neuroscience proposes for head-direction cells. a perturbation off the ring decays back to it, while a shift in position along the ring is held rather than erased. rotating the ring plane changes the model's predicted observation to the one for the rotated heading, at a gain of 0.897 and an R-squared of 0.998, so the variable is used causally and not just correlated with heading. the shuffled-action null also steers, at a gain of 0.735, so the separation between the conditions rests on fidelity rather than on steering alone.

this matters because a ring shows up in almost every one of these models, so anyone reading structure off a hidden state could point at it and call it a heading representation. the fidelity test under blackout and the causal rotation are what separate a variable the model actually integrates and uses from one that only looks right, and only the partial-observability condition produces the genuine one.

Finding 1

with no pose supervision, a predictive world model builds a heading variable that tracks true heading at a fidelity of 0.99 only under partial observability, while the best always-on control reaches 0.61 and the 36 models separate with no overlap, and the variable is a causally steerable continuous attractor.

a probe can decode a coordinate the dynamics never use

Robotics World Modeling @ NeurIPS 2026Poster

the heading result raised a sharper question. a probe can read a variable out of a model's latent state even when the model's own transition dynamics never act on it, so decoding and use are different properties. to tell them apart i built a label-free test that uses only actions and the frozen model's own predictions. a candidate coordinate is a plane in the latent space, and the test asks whether rotating the state inside that plane reproduces the effect of an extra action, judged against a variance-matched null. in the shuffled-action control from the heading study, heading decodes to within 20 to 24 degrees, yet the conjugate plane sits 69 to 83 degrees away, so the variable is present in a decaying trace of the last observation while the dynamics do not act on it.

which coordinates a model builds is decided by the observation, not the actuators. i trained the same model on three two-joint arms that differ only in how the image depends on the joints, with dynamics, actions, and training budget held fixed. the coordinates follow what the image makes separable. a serial arm, where the image depends on one combination of the joints, gives one coordinate at (1,0). a parity arm, where the image depends on the sum, gives one at (1,1). a decoupled arm, where the image depends on both joints separately, gives two, at (1,0) and (0,1). both joint angles decode in every case, but the proximal joint's conjugacy margin is six times the distal joint's, so extra actuators do not buy extra coordinates.

Finding

decoding and use come apart. a coordinate can be read from a world model's latent state while its dynamics ignore it, and the coordinates the dynamics do use are set by what the observation makes separable, not by the number of actuators. this is shown in small synthetic settings with planar rotations, not on physical robots or video.

a fair baseline erases the retention advantage of local learning rules

Blog

biologically inspired learning rules are often reported to forget less than backpropagation, so i tested whether the advantage survives a fair comparison on class-incremental Split-CIFAR-10. a rule that learns each new task less well also has less to forget, so i first calibrated an instrument. weakening backpropagation along a ladder of settings makes backward transfer almost a deterministic function of learning accuracy, with an R-squared of 0.995, which sets how small a difference i can resolve, a held-out error of 1.09 points. i then scored each rule against backpropagation at the same learning accuracy, tuning the baseline as hard as the rule. feedback alignment and direct feedback alignment were indistinguishable from backpropagation, predictive coding retained 0.21 points more with a 95% interval of [+0.11, +0.31], about a fifth of the instrument's own error, and replay added 10.6 points. so any retention advantage from locality on this protocol is under a third of a point.

i care about this beyond neuroscience because continued training of a deployed model, whether fine-tuning or reinforcement learning from human feedback, can erase capabilities and safety behaviors the model had before. that is catastrophic forgetting with a deployment address, and the one constraint engram adds is that any method claiming to protect earlier behavior has to be compared at matched learning of the new task.

Finding 2

once learning accuracy is matched and the baseline is tuned fairly, the retention advantage of local rules is under a third of a point while replay adds 10.6, so locality itself does not reduce forgetting on this protocol.

Takeaways

what both studies share

pressure builds the variable
a steerable heading variable appears at fidelity 0.99 only when a blackout forces the model to integrate.
it is a genuine attractor
off-ring perturbations decay, a phase shift along the ring persists, and rotation is causal.
a fair baseline is everything
matched learning erases the local-rule retention edge, leaving under a third of a point.
forgetting is an alignment problem
continued training erases old behavior the same way it erases an old task.

Limitations

the heading study is in simulation on a planar robot with range sensors, so whether the same faithful variable appears from richer sensing or on hardware is untested here. the continual-learning result is scoped to class-incremental Split-CIFAR-10 with a growing head, which sits near a retention floor, and a wider-window replication is the next step. the engram code is not public yet, so the Code link is held until it is released.
