Abstract

a protein fitness proxy is a computable stand-in for a slow wet-lab measurement, and a design campaign ranks thousands of candidates by it and tests the top ones. gauntlet stress-tests a proxy before anyone ranks designs with it, by asking whether it beats a trivial baseline on measured outcomes, stays useful in the top-ranked region a search actually selects, and resists being driven to pathological inputs. on 504 measured PET-hydrolase variants across 25 studies, an ESM-2 650M zero-shot score ranks activity at a within-study Spearman of 0.01, a substitution matrix is negative, and a plain count of mutations reaches 0.25. a score that cannot beat counting edits is not measuring function on the task it is sold for.

a score that cannot beat counting

the simplest bar a proxy has to clear is a trivial baseline, and the clearest trivial baseline for a mutant is how many mutations it carries. on PET-hydrolytic activity, a zero-shot ESM-2 650M log-likelihood score ranks variants at a within-study Spearman of 0.01, a substitution matrix scores below zero, a hydropathy feature does better, and a plain mutation count reaches 0.25. the expensive model is the weakest of the four scorers on the property a design campaign would use it for.

Finding 1

a zero-shot protein language model ranks measured PETase activity at a within-study Spearman of 0.01, worse than a mutation count at 0.25, so it adds nothing over a trivial baseline on the task it is used for. a proxy that cannot clear counting is not evidence about function, whatever its benchmark correlation elsewhere.

why run the audit before the campaign, not after?

because the proxy is the score people reach for first when they rank designs without measuring them, and the moment a search starts choosing the candidates it likes best, the proxy is steering the campaign rather than describing it. the three gauntlet checks, beat a trivial baseline, stay useful in the top-ranked tail, and resist an optimizer, are the questions whose answers decide whether the first ranked batch is worth synthesizing. this axis reports the first check; the others are in the broader evaluations program.

what it adds up to

counting is the bar
a mutation count ranks measured activity at 0.25; a zero-shot protein score reaches 0.01, so it has to earn its cost against counting first.
within study, not across
the comparison is inside each study, so the gap is not an artifact of pooling incomparable assays.
audit before you rank
a proxy becomes an objective the moment a search uses it, so the check belongs before the first batch, not after.

Limitations. this axis reports one property, PET-hydrolytic activity, with a second on melting temperature, drawn from published measured variants; it is a statement about these assays and these scorers, not a universal ranking of protein models. zero-shot log-likelihood is one way to score a model, and a supervised or embedding-based readout can do better. the top-tail and reward-hacking checks that complete the gauntlet battery live in the broader evaluations work and are not re-reported here.
