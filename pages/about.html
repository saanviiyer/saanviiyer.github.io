About Me

Hello! I'm Saanvi, an undergraduate at Caltech studying Computation and Neural Systems with a minor in Math and Information Science, advised by Markus Meister. I like problems that require the languages of several fields at once: the math to formalize them, the experiments to ground them, and the computation to make them tractable.

The through-line in my work is making complex biological systems legible. Biological measurements are noisy and partial: an MRI shows anatomy but not electrical activity; an EEG captures timing but localizes poorly; a protein sequence does not announce what a mutation will do. I build models that combine those views, learn useful representations, and reveal where the evidence is still thin.

Right now, that takes several forms. At Stanford Medicine, I pair pediatric brain MRI with EEG to improve epileptic-lesion detection. In Caltech's Shimojo Lab, I work with focused ultrasound, EEG, and fMRI in studies of noninvasive brain stimulation. In the Perona Lab, I align 3D kidney CT scans with genomic data; at Seattle Children's, I modeled developmental lineages in medulloblastoma. Different organs and modalities, same habit of mind: connect measurements that can explain one another.

My north star is closed-loop neuromodulation: systems that read neural activity, infer what state a circuit is in, and adapt their response in real time. I am not building that entire loop yet. My current work is about earning the pieces it would require—better multimodal representations, clearer biological interpretation, and a realistic account of model reliability. My protein-language-model work belongs in that story as a parallel test bed: it lets me study whether a learned representation reflects biology or merely rewards the proxy we chose.

I also care about making technical communities easier to enter. I founded and led a SWENext chapter, mentored students through hackathons and outreach, and now help lead Caltech's Neurotechers and Neuro/ML communities. I enjoy turning a specialized idea into something other people can question, build on, and feel invited into.
