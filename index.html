AI × neuroscience × biological systems

Saanvi S. Subramanian

Undergraduate at Caltech, studying Computation and Neural Systems with a minor in Math and Information Science, advised by Markus Meister.

I build machine-learning systems that make complex biology more legible. Across brain imaging, neural signals, cellular data, and proteins, I ask the same question: how can we recover useful structure from noisy measurements without losing sight of what the model can—and cannot—tell us?

My north star is adaptive neural interfaces: systems that can read the brain, reason about its state, and respond precisely enough to help.

The research arc

Measure a living system. Model what matters. Move carefully toward intervention.

001

Make the signal legible

Combine complementary measurements—MRI with EEG, CT with genomics, cells with developmental context—so each modality resolves what the others miss.

002

Interrogate the model

Study the representations a model learns, test whether its proxies remain trustworthy, and distinguish a useful biological signal from a convenient shortcut.

003

Close the loop

Use reliable representations to guide experiments and, eventually, interventions. For the brain, that means adaptive systems whose next action depends on the state they observe.
