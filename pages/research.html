research

I study when learned representations and scoring systems remain trustworthy once they are used to make decisions. I work across protein design, world models, and neuroscience, where a plausible benchmark can fail in the exact regime that matters.

Reliable AI for biological design

i test whether protein models encode the biological property a design process needs, especially after a proxy becomes an objective. the recurring pattern is to start with trivial baselines and confound controls, then measure what happens in the top-ranked region an optimizer would actually use.

PRISM: i built a pipeline that applies all 78 COSMIC single-base-substitution signatures to protein-coding sequences and measures how each mutant cloud moves through ESM-2 space relative to Pfam domain centroids. SBS17a repeatedly steered sequences toward PF17041 across two independent Pfam clans. the follow-up controls changed my interpretation: a composition-matched control reproduced 72% of the movement, and protein length explained 92.5% of displacement. the project became as much about auditing the representation as reading the signal.

gauntlet: gauntlet stress-tests protein fitness proxies before they are used to rank designs. on measured PETase activity, a plain mutation-count baseline beat ESM-2 zero-shot. the broader audit asks whether a proxy adds value beyond trivial baselines, stays useful in the top-ranked tail, and resists reward hacking.

action identifiability in protein-design agents: this project asks whether an agent's apparent success identifies the biological action that caused it. the goal is to separate good outcomes from justified decisions.

biologos: biologos tests whether genomic-to-protein transfer survives direct controls. in the current evaluation, the apparent protein-fitness signal from a genomic language model was indistinguishable from a substitution-matrix baseline.

plimsoll: plimsoll turns evaluation discipline into software. it checks a research repository for trivial baselines, verified nulls, decisive controls, named confounds, and printed denominators, then quotes the evidence behind each verdict.

World models and learning

i study when a model builds a usable internal variable from partial observations, and how to tell whether that variable actually supports prediction or action.

faithful heading: predictive world models trained from pixels recovered a faithful heading variable without pose supervision, but only under partial observability. i ran these simulations to understand when missing observations force a model to maintain a stable internal state instead of relying on the current frame.

task-space reward learning: with researchers at Google DeepMind and the University of Edinburgh, i fit recurrent neural networks to behavior in a 487-participant bandit task space.

surgical video models: i test whether video foundation models use temporal information when scoring surgical movement. a single still frame matched the full 16-frame video across the current encoders. the next experiments isolate order, change, and static appearance so the representation claim can be falsified.

engram: engram is a controlled test of biologically inspired continual-learning rules. it asks whether an apparent retention gain remains after training exposure and compute are matched.

AI for neuroscience and medicine

i build and evaluate models around the information a clinician actually uses, then test whether each modality changes the decision rather than only improving a benchmark.

CortSeer: combines MRI with clinical and EEG findings to re-examine subtle epileptogenic lesions. i built the project during a Caltech SURF in Stanford Pediatric Neuroradiology. presented orally at the ECCV AI for Medical 3D Vision workshop.

radiology: three threads, all in progress: a study of what label-degradation experiments actually measure (in preparation for CVPR 2027, not submitted), a radiogenomic CLIP model linking 3D kidney-tumor CT with gene mutations at the Caltech Computer Vision Lab, and interpreting genomic representations.
