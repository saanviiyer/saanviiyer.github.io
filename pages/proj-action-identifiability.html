Abstract

Molecular design agents choose interventions by scoring predicted outcomes, which only works if the scorer distinguishes the actions available to the policy. I test that requirement directly, using 78 COSMIC mutational processes as the action space and distance in ESM-2 representation space as the score. Median pairwise distance falls from 0.815 between native mutational spectra to 0.0068 between ESM-2 outcome regions, a 120× compression, and a composition-matched control reproduces the destinations. A learned policy names the true action in 7.55% of held-out cases, and split-conformal prediction reaches 92.2% coverage only by returning 66.8 of 78 actions on average. At 100 scorer calls, adaptive action selection reaches mean target distance 0.177, against 0.165 for a fixed policy and 0.173 for a random one, and an independent Pfam profile HMM finds no PF17041 hits at the curated threshold. Biological admissibility and scorer-level action identifiability are different properties. An agent should test whether its scorer separates candidate actions before it reports a mechanism.

the setup

A closed-loop molecular agent proposes an intervention, scores the molecule it produces, and uses that score to choose the next action. The loop searches large spaces efficiently, but only when the scorer resolves the actions available to the policy. If several interventions produce indistinguishable scored outcomes, the agent cannot tell which one caused a result, and the mechanism it reports afterwards is a guess. I call this requirement action identifiability: whether the agent's own scoring model distinguishes the outcomes of competing actions.

Identifiability is not biological admissibility. An action can represent a valid biological process while the scorer discards exactly the information that separates it from the others. The two properties come apart, and most pipelines check only one of them.

the testbed needs an action space whose ground-truth distinctions are known independently of any learned model. COSMIC single-base-substitution signatures give that. each signature is a distribution over 96 trinucleotide substitution types, catalogued from a real mutational process, in its own model-free parameterization. an action is a signature. executing it on a coding sequence samples compatible substitutions and produces a mutant, which i translate, embed with ESM-2, and score by cosine distance to a target Pfam-domain centroid, lower being better. that gives both halves of the comparison: how far apart two actions are as processes, and how far apart their outcomes are after scoring. the score measures movement in representation space. it does not measure protein function or acquisition of the target domain, and that distinction turns out to carry the paper.

distinct processes reach the same place

across all 3,003 pairs of the 78 signatures, median distance between native 96-channel spectra is 0.815 and median distance between their ESM-2 outcome regions is 0.0068. the scored action space is roughly two orders of magnitude tighter than the space the actions actually live in. 1,780 pairs, 59.3%, are more than 0.5 apart in COSMIC space and fall within 0.011 of each other after scoring. SBS1 and SBS7a, clock-like ageing and UV exposure, are 0.952 apart as spectra and 0.0055 apart as outcomes.

what the composition control shows ESM-2 separates marginal substitution composition more strongly than trinucleotide context.

scrambling trinucleotide context and mutation placement while preserving the realized substitution composition reproduces each action's ESM-2 destination to within cosine 0.02. at the four-signature scale the same control moves the destination by 0.0081 ± 0.0016 against a seed-to-seed floor of 0.0014 ± 0.0005, so context does register, 5.8× above the sampling floor, but the effect is 3.6 to 7.3× smaller than the separation between signatures with different substitution composition. the three collapsed processes are all C>T dominated and SBS17a is T>C dominated, which is the composition axis rather than the biology.

Finding 1

across all 3,003 pairs, biologically distinct mutational processes often reach similar ESM-2 destinations, and the separation that survives tracks broad substitution composition rather than the trinucleotide context that defines a signature. an agent in this representation should return the compatible set or abstain, not name one process.

Finding 2

the measurement procedure did much of the work. stop-at-first-stop translation turns 524 of 2,028 simulated mutants, 25.8%, into one-residue placeholders, and per-signature placeholder count alone explains R² = 0.683 of pairwise destination distance at 8M and 0.870 at 650M. a length control removes the artifact, but the binary collapse rate is then strongly scale-dependent, 23.8% of pairs at or below the 0.011 threshold at 8M against 72.5% at 650M. i report both and do not pick one as a catalogue-wide rate.

controls remove the gains

on the PF17041 target the structured action looks like it works. it reduces ESM-2 distance by 0.0736 ± 0.0030 across five seeds against 0.0218 ± 0.0051 for edit-count-matched random mutation, an improvement of 0.0519 with paired-bootstrap 95% CI [0.0477, 0.0573]. that is the result a design paper would report and stop. then composition matching erases it.

the paired difference between the structured action and its composition-matched control is −0.0027, 95% CI [−0.0063, 0.0012]. there is no evidence that trinucleotide context or mutation placement, the things that actually define a mutational signature, add steering power beyond the substitution composition ESM-2 already preserves.

scorer feedback adds nothing either. an unselected-drift control scores every proposal and accepts it regardless of the score. it improves target distance by 0.0737 against 0.0736 under Metropolis selection, and for the random arm 0.0236 unselected against 0.0218 selected. whatever the loop gains, it gains from the proposal distribution rather than from the feedback.

adaptive selection does not pay for itself

giving the agent more candidates to evaluate does not rescue it. at 100 ESM-2 scorer calls, five-action one-step lookahead reaches mean target distance 0.177 against 0.165 for a fixed action and 0.173 for random selection. adaptive is worse than fixed by +0.0113, 95% CI [+0.0082, +0.0143], and worse than random by +0.0042, 95% CI [+0.0021, +0.0057]. lookahead spends scorer calls on comparisons the scorer cannot resolve and pays for them in lost transitions. evaluating more candidate actions does not improve optimization when the feedback carries too little usable information.

Finding 3

every apparent gain in this loop survives only until a matched control is added. composition matching removes the advantage of the structured action, unselected drift removes the advantage of scorer feedback, and an equal scorer-call budget removes the advantage of adaptive selection. the arm that looks like steering is doing the work of its own substitution composition.

what the policy can and cannot do

a supervised policy trained on 35,403 realized Pfam-domain transitions predicts which of the 78 signatures produced a given transition. on a protein-grouped split it reaches 7.55% top-1, against 6.12% for a nearest-neighbor baseline and 1.28% for uniform chance. measured against a train-only per-(origin, target) frequency lookup the edge is +0.93 percentage points, 95% CI [+0.71, +1.27], and top-5 is 19.65% against 16.93%. the policy carries real information about action choice and still predicts the wrong action in 92.45% of held-out cases.

it also does not generalize to unseen actions. holding out complete COSMIC processes drops top-1 to 0.8%, below the 1.3% uniform baseline, and holding out SBS17a yields 0% recovery. the model interpolates among action classes it saw in training rather than learning what a signature is.

set-valued prediction raises coverage by returning most of the catalogue. a direct ESM-2 neighborhood reaches 82.6% coverage at mean set size 58.2. adaptive prediction sets reach 92.2% coverage and return 66.8 actions on average, with median size 78, the entire catalogue. abstention produces smaller outputs, but retaining 8.6% of test cases yields only 33.0% conditional accuracy. this is what a non-identifiable action space looks like from the inside: the uncertainty quantification is not malfunctioning, it is correctly reporting that the evidence does not single out an action.

decoding is not identifiability

the representation is not empty, and it is worth being precise about that. a probe that reads an ESM-2 displacement and names which of five processes produced it reaches 66% five-way accuracy against 20% chance, and 89% on SBS17a. the structure of the errors is the finding. the confusions are not random, and SBS1 is confused with SBS2 even though their COSMIC spectra are near-orthogonal at cosine 0.02. the representation decodes a real signal and still merges processes the catalogue separates.

Finding 4

a readable signal is not a separable action. the probe reads the enacting process at 66%, yet the agent's own loop merges processes the catalogue holds apart, and the interface at the end of this is a compatible set or an abstention rather than a named mechanism.

validating outside the objective

the optimization moves proteins measurably closer to the PF17041 centroid, and the obvious question is whether that means anything biologically. an independent Pfam profile HMM, evaluated with HMMER 3 against the PF17041.11 SasG E profile, answers it: no evaluated sequence passes Pfam's curated gathering threshold. under a permissive reporting threshold none of the five structured-action sequences produces a match at all, and other arms produce only weak, non-significant hits with best E = 0.34.

The objective is also exploitable. An agent making unconstrained single-residue edits drives cosine distance to the PF17041 centroid from 0.26 to 0.009 over 200 edits. I report that against the minimum rather than the mean of a natural-protein reference set, because the standard deviation is not identified here: the reference set behind the figure I previously reported cannot be reconstructed, and recomputing the band on every available input gives 0.227 ± 0.084, 0.216 ± 0.078 and 0.281 ± 0.073, which would place the endpoint 2.6, 2.7 or 3.7 standard deviations below depending on a choice nobody recorded. The endpoint does sit below the closest approach of all 247 natural proteins I can measure, 0.105, and an order of magnitude below their mean of 0.216. ESM-2 naturalness falls over the same trajectory from −0.406 to −0.474, which is 0.9 standard deviations from that reference mean and therefore inside the natural band, not below it. The plausibility signal I removed does detect the departure, but the objective and that self-check both live in ESM-2, so passing a check in the same representation is not independent evidence.

Finding 5

movement under the ESM-2 objective is supported; acquisition of PF17041-like sequence properties is not. these are different claims, and only the first is measured by the thing the agent optimizes.

why does the 0.011 threshold need this much hedging?

because it was defined from the SBS1 to SBS7a distance in the original four-signature 8M uncontrolled experiment, and it does not carry the same meaning elsewhere. it contains the SBS1, SBS7a and SBS2 reference cluster in two of the four model-by-readout conditions and splits it in the other two. picking the condition where it behaves after seeing the result would overstate collapse, so the catalogue-wide conclusions rest on continuous distances and within-process spread instead.

what it adds up to

distinct actions become indistinguishable
biological grounding does not guarantee scorer-level identifiability. COSMIC describes distinct processes and ESM-2 maps many of their outcomes to nearly the same place.
decoding is not identifiability
a probe reads the enacting process at 66%, yet the dynamics merge processes the catalogue holds apart, so a readable signal is not a separable action.
the separation is composition
what little distance remains tracks C>T versus T>C substitution composition, not the trinucleotide context that names a signature.
agency does not overcome poor resolution
adaptive selection, lookahead and uncertainty quantification cannot recover a distinction the scorer has already discarded, and under equal budget lookahead is worse than a fixed action.
measure the representation, not the agent
a translation artifact alone explains up to R² 0.870 of the apparent distance, so the right unit of analysis is the representation's separability rather than the agent's hit rate.
same-representation validation is not independent
the objective and its self-check both live in ESM-2, so passing the check is not evidence the action was the right one.

the three-step evaluation this implies

  • measure whether the scorer distinguishes the actions available to the policy. compare distances in the action's own parameterization against distances between scored outcomes. it is cheap and it is diagnostic.
  • compare optimization against controls matched for proposal distribution, edit count, sequence length and scorer-call budget. each of these removed a result that would otherwise have been reported.
  • validate the molecular claim with a measurement independent of the optimization objective. if the objective and the check share a representation, passing the check is not evidence.

if the scorer cannot resolve competing actions, the right behavior is to return a useful uncertainty set or abstain, not to name a mechanism.

Next steps

  • Advance PRISM into a deployable RL environment. The design loop already has what most biological RL environments lack: an objective I have shown to be gameable, and a referee that does not share the objective's representation. That makes it a benchmark for reward hacking rather than a design tool, and the negative result above is what validates it. The scope decision comes first, because an environment that accepts an arbitrary target is a different artifact from a measurement with a fixed one.
  • Test at larger scale, with more compute and more data. Every result here rests on ESM-2 8M and 650M, which share training data and tokenisation, so the scale comparison is not an independent replication. Whether the collapse persists in a model family trained differently is the open question, and it is the one that decides how far these conclusions travel.

Limitations. the steering and decoding experiments cover a subset of signatures while the destination analysis covers all 78, so catalogue-wide overlap is established and per-signature steering specificity is not. ESM-2 8M and 650M share training data and tokenisation, so the scale comparison is not an independent-model replication. the 0.011 collapse bar is inherited from an earlier four-signature experiment and does not carry identical meaning across every model-by-readout condition, so the catalogue-wide conclusions rest on continuous distances rather than on that threshold. the unconstrained optimisation result shows this objective can be exploited under single-residue search, not that every constrained design objective fails the same way. conclusions apply to the action–scorer pair studied here; other molecular actions or scorers may preserve more mechanistic information, which is the point of making it a measurable question rather than an assumption. methods that steer protein representations are dual-use, so the action space is restricted to characterised processes and the work audits failure modes rather than claiming successful molecular design.
