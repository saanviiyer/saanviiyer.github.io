when we evaluate an AI agent doing biology, are we measuring whether it found something true, or only whether it scored well on the signal we happened to hand it?

Abstract

an evaluation is almost always reported as an average over a dataset and then used to make a decision about a small subset: which candidate to advance, which action to take, which variant to synthesise. those are different quantities, and the gap between them is where these three threads live. the first asks whether the thing that certifies a scorer can be trusted, and finds a gate that rewards a scorer which memorised the answers at AUROC 0.991 while it performs at random where it matters. the second asks whether the representation an agent acts through can tell its own candidate actions apart, and finds that it partly cannot, at a decoding accuracy well above chance but with errors concentrated exactly where the underlying processes are most distinct. the third asks whether the statistic the field reports predicts the decision it is used for, and finds the squared rank association falling from 0.50 at a top-10% cutoff to 0.16 at top-1%, with three assays doing worse than random selection despite positive reported correlations. the common structure is that each failure is invisible to the summary normally reported and only appears at the point where something is chosen. whether that makes them one finding or three applications of one method is argued at the end of this page, and the honest answer is closer to the second.

01, The Challenge

the evaluation is the part nobody evaluates

an agent doing biology is judged by a signal: a verifier's verdict, a benchmark correlation, a validation score computed in some representation. the agent gets scrutinised and the signal does not. but the signal is where the assumption lives. a verifier can certify a scorer that has memorised the answers. a benchmark correlation can be genuine and still fail to predict the decision anyone makes from it. a validation score computed in the same representation the agent optimises is not independent evidence. in each case the arithmetic is right and the conclusion is wrong, and the failure is invisible from the headline number.

The central question

do our ways of evaluating AI agents in biology actually measure what we claim?

biology is where this is checkable. deep mutational scans record the experimental outcome for nearly every candidate in a pool, so an agent's decisions can be replayed and its counterfactual actions scored directly. that makes the domain a place where a verifier can be held against ground truth rather than against another model's opinion. all three posters exploit that, and the evaluations line of work is where the same control battery gets applied to single model scores.

complete-outcome domain a task where nearly every available action already has a recorded experimental result.

this is the property that makes the verifier itself measurable. without it, a verifier's silence is indistinguishable from a verifier that works, because there is nothing to contradict it.

selection value the fraction of achievable gain captured by acting on a model's top-ranked candidates.

an oracle ranking gives 1 and random selection gives 0. this is the quantity a design campaign actually receives, and it is not the quantity a benchmark reports.

action-space identifiability whether the candidate actions an agent chooses among reach distinguishable destinations in its representation.

if two hypotheses drive the system to the same representational state, no accuracy metric or calibration curve can recover a distinction the representation does not contain.

02, The Approach

attack the signal, not the agent

all three threads make the same move: take the signal an evaluation actually runs on, construct the adversarial or control condition that signal cannot distinguish, and score both against recorded outcomes rather than against each other. what differs is where the move is aimed. the verifier is what certifies a scorer; the representation is what an agent acts through; the metric is what the field reports. those three sit at decreasing distance from the decision, which is why a failure can pass through all of them undetected, and that ordering is the closest thing here to a stack. it is worth being careful about the claim, though. three levels chosen after the fact, in one scientific domain, is not a demonstrated decomposition of the evaluation pipeline; it is three places the same test was run, which happen to line up. the stronger statement is methodological rather than structural: wherever a reported number summarises behaviour over a distribution and a decision consumes only its tail, the two can be measured separately, and here they came apart every time they were.

03, Results

three posters, one standard of evidence

when the verifier is wrong

when the verifier is wrong: red-teaming agent verification where ground truth exists

NeurIPS Verify-Agents 2026Sole author

[TODO: Saanvi to add the Verify-Agents OpenReview link once the decision is out.]

this is the poster that red-teams the verifier itself. a closed-loop protein-design agent picks candidates from a pool of thousands, and a trust gate decides whether to delegate that ranking to a supplied scorer by checking the scorer's AUROC against the outcomes collected so far. the gate never verifies that the supplied scorer is actually parameter-free, which is the whole vulnerability. 41 deep mutational scans of 1,800 to 7,900 candidates each, plus five published multi-round campaigns, drawn from ProteinGym, FLIP, and PETML.

  1. the leakage attack a scorer that memorises the observed outcomes and outputs noise everywhere else. across 4,920 trials, a fully leaked scorer reaches gate AUROC 0.991 and passes 100% of trials, against 0.663 and 77.2% for a truthful zero-shot scorer. it ranks above the useful scorer and performs at random on the unmeasured pool, costing about 1.05 good candidates per batch. even 25% leakage passes 77% of the time. optimising against this gate rewards memorisation.
  2. forward validation, and why it is not enough score the scorer only on measurements collected after it was supplied. acquisition order creates the holdout, so the gate never has to inspect the scorer's implementation. this removes the leakage advantage: the truthful scorer holds at 0.687 and 79.5% while the fully leaked scorer falls to 0.495 and 34.8%, which is the finite-sample false-pass floor rather than residual signal.
  3. the pool-side attack forward validation cannot see leave every measured candidate untouched and corrupt only the unmeasured pool. because the measured scores are unchanged, both gates return identical statistics to the truthful scorer by construction, 0.663 and 0.676. the selections lose 1.24 good candidates per batch. no gate that reads only previously measured candidates can detect this.
  4. top-pick probing, and probe-aware evasion measure a prefix of the scorer's own top picks before committing the batch. a 25% probe detects 99.2% of naive pool-side attacks while costing the truthful scorer 4.4% of its yield. an attacker who baits the top of the ranking to match the probe drops detection to 45.9%, and to 39.6% at a 50% probe.

Finding 1

a trust gate that evaluates a supplied scorer in sample certifies a scorer that has memorised the evaluation outcomes. it ranks that scorer above a truthful one at AUROC 0.991 against 0.663, passes it in every trial, and the agent loses about 1.05 good candidates per batch for following it.

Finding 2

forward validation closes the leakage surface and nothing else. an attacker restricted to the unmeasured pool produces gate statistics identical to the truthful scorer by construction and costs 1.24 good candidates per batch. detecting it requires spending experimental budget on candidates the scorer currently recommends.

Finding 3

detection is not recovery. the probe catches nearly every naive pool-side attack but recovers only about 10% of the lost value when the fallback is random selection, moving the agent from −0.162 to −0.036 where the truthful scorer would have given +1.082. an in-house ridge model trained on the campaign's own 48 measurements recovers 17% at a 25% probe and 28% at a 50% probe, and overtakes the supplied scorer entirely after roughly 115 measurements, reaching +1.745 against +0.741 at 384.

why does detection without a fallback fail?

because if being caught sends the agent to random selection, and the attacker's goal was already to reduce the agent to random, detection imposes almost no cost on the attacker. a verifier needs a fallback that preserves value, which means an independent source of capability outside the compromised scorer's control. verification can tell you when you need that. it cannot supply it.

the poster also reports four further verifier pathologies measured against ground truth, a sampled stochastic baseline that biases the comparison toward itself (decision accuracy 43.1% to 56.9% once the expectation is used), verifier selection that does not survive held-out tasks (the incumbent rule moves from second to last across 35 held-out scans), a protocol mismatch worth about a fifth of the observed gap, and a gate statistic whose resolution cannot support its own threshold (fold enrichment moves in discrete steps and is exactly zero for 34.3% of estimates in steps of 1.92 over six attainable values, of which five are ever realised). a fifth pathology appeared on 13 tasks and disappears across all 41, so it is withdrawn in the appendix.

Limitations. the evaluation is offline and counterfactual on pools that earlier experimental choices already selected, so differences between policies are interpretable and absolute performance is not a population estimate. the policy-transfer experiment covers six non-LLM policy classes and shows that damage follows the fraction of action selection delegated to the compromised scorer, with the gate reading 0.6632 under both conditions and changing its decision in 0 of 1,640 trials. transfer to an LLM-driven research agent is untested and the mapping in the limitations section is conceptual. representational identifiability

representational identifiability limits action selection in a protein-design agent

NeurIPS AgenticLS 2026Sole author

[TODO: Saanvi to add the AgenticLS OpenReview link. also decide whether this page or the action-identifiability stub is the canonical home for this thread, right now the stub says MoML 2026 and this says AgenticLS, and both cannot be the whole story.]

the previous thread asks whether the verifier can be trusted. this one asks something earlier: whether the representation the agent acts through can tell its candidate answers apart at all. the testbed is a hypothesise-simulate-validate loop for mutational-driver discovery, using ESM-2 and the 78 COSMIC somatic mutation signatures. the agent proposes a mutational process, enacts its mean hidden-state displacement by activation steering, and compares the enacted direction against the displacement the measured mutation caused. if two processes reach the same destination, no amount of calibration recovers the difference.

three biologically distinct processes, SBS1 (clock-like), SBS7a (UV), SBS2 (APOBEC), land at pairwise cosine distance at or below 0.011, while SBS17a sits out at 0.04 to 0.08. the catalogue confusion is not just COSMIC's own structure: SBS2 and SBS7a do have similar spectra at cosine 0.71, but SBS2 and SBS1 are near-orthogonal in COSMIC at cosine 0.02 and still merge in ESM-2. the representation is collapsing a distinction the catalogue makes.

what the composition control shows ESM-2 separates marginal substitution composition more strongly than trinucleotide context.

scrambling trinucleotide context while preserving each substitution type's marginal probability moves the destination by 0.0081 ± 0.0016, against a seed-to-seed floor of 0.0014 ± 0.0005. so context does register, 5.8× above the sampling floor, but the effect is 3.6 to 7.3× smaller than the separation between signatures with different substitution composition. the three collapsed processes are all C>T dominated and SBS17a is T>C dominated, which is the composition axis, not the biology.

Finding 4

across all 3,003 pairs of the 78 COSMIC signatures, biologically distinct mutational processes often reach similar ESM-2 destinations, and the separation that does exist tracks broad substitution composition rather than the trinucleotide context that defines the signatures. an agent in this representation should return the compatible set or abstain, not name one process.

Finding 5

the measurement procedure was doing much of the work. stop-at-first-stop translation turns 524 of 2,028 simulated mutants (25.8%) into one-residue placeholders, and per-signature placeholder count alone explains R² = 0.683 of pairwise destination distance at 8M and 0.870 at 650M. length control removes the artifact, but the binary collapse rate then becomes strongly scale-dependent: 23.8% of pairs at or below the 0.011 threshold at 8M against 72.5% at 650M. i report both and do not pick one as a catalogue-wide rate.

Finding 6

the embedding-proximity objective is exploitable, and the validation check shares its representation. an agent making unconstrained single-residue edits drives cosine distance to the PF17041 centroid from 0.26 to 0.009 over 200 edits, landing 5.0 standard deviations below the natural-protein reference mean of 0.194 ± 0.037 across 25 measured proteins, while ESM-2 naturalness falls from −0.41 to −0.50, 2.2 sd below the natural band. the removed plausibility signal does detect the departure. but because both the objective and the causal self-validation score live in ESM-2, validation in the same representation is not independent evidence against this failure.

why does the 0.011 threshold need this much hedging?

because it was defined from the SBS1–SBS7a distance in the original four-signature 8M uncontrolled experiment, and it does not carry the same meaning elsewhere. it contains the SBS1/SBS7a/SBS2 reference cluster in two of the four model-by-readout conditions and splits it in the other two. picking the condition where it behaves after seeing the result would overstate collapse, so the catalogue-wide conclusion rests on continuous distances and within-process spread instead.

Limitations. the steering and decoding experiments cover a subset of signatures while the destination analysis covers all 78, so catalogue-wide overlap is established and per-signature steering specificity is not. ESM-2 8M and 650M share training data and tokenisation, so the scale comparison is not an independent-model replication. the unconstrained optimisation result shows this objective can be exploited under single-residue search, not that every constrained design objective fails the same way. a matched-compute control also erases the apparent agency gain: at 100 scorer calls, fixed and random search reach final centroid distances of 0.1586 and 0.1585 against 0.1625 for the adaptive policy, so the adaptive policy has no measured advantage on those 20 protein-goal tasks. that control does not touch the identifiability result, but it is why the release ledger has this poster as position-only.

does benchmark correlation predict selection quality?

does benchmark correlation predict selection quality for protein design?

NeurIPS Trust-AI-Eval 2026NeurIPS GEM-Bio 2026Sole author

[TODO: Saanvi to add both OpenReview links, and to say on the page how the two submissions differ rather than letting a reader assume they are the same poster twice. as drafted, TAE carries the top-1%/top-10% cutoff decay across 60 units and the label-free diagnostic, and GEM-Bio carries the top-20% cutoff all four corpora support, the PETML near-zero result, and the Rubisco controls. the release ledger has GEM-Bio on venue hold, so the split may not survive.]

the first two threads attack a verifier and a representation. this one attacks the number the field actually reports. protein benchmarks report Spearman correlation between model scores and measured outcomes, which summarises ranking quality over an entire assay. a design campaign does something else: it takes the top few candidates and tests them. those are different quantities, and the question is how much the first tells you about the second. 70 units across four corpora, 41 ProteinGym scans, 16 SSMuLA combinatorial libraries, three Rubisco kinetic axes from 8,760 single mutants, and 10 PETML plastic-degradation campaigns.

0.50 → 0.16
variance in selection value explained by reported correlation, from the top 10% (ρ = +0.707) to the top 1% (ρ = +0.404), across 60 units
Finding 7

correlation becomes less informative exactly where campaigns act. it explains half the variance in top-10% selection value and 16% at top-1%. three of the 41 ProteinGym assays produce worse-than-random top-1% selections despite positive reported correlations, the worst at selection value −0.300 on a reported ρ of +0.105.

Finding 8

the same reported correlation means different things in different benchmark families. at matched correlation, SSMuLA libraries deliver +0.265 more top-1% selection value than ProteinGym assays (Mann–Whitney p = 0.0001), and +0.147 at top-10% (p = 0.0022). a second scorer family reproduces the offset at +0.252 (p = 0.0003). so an aggregate leaderboard score mixes model behaviour with benchmark composition, and two methods evaluated on different family mixtures are not comparable through the reported scalar alone.

Finding 9

the obvious label-free fix does not transfer. score-distribution skew looks promising retrospectively, correlating with residual top-1% selection value at +0.633 across 60 units and +0.489 within ProteinGym, but its sign reverses across families: fitted on ProteinGym and applied to SSMuLA it gives −0.388, and the reverse direction gives −0.582. a fourth corpus reverses it again, −0.783 within PETML against +0.551 within ProteinGym. skew adds information when it accompanies the reported correlation, and fails as a standalone transferable rule.

Finding 10

the critique has a boundary, and reporting it is part of the result. where a supervised model has genuine signal, correlation and selection improve together: across 12 assays and three cross-validation schemes on identical folds, mean selection gain is +0.035 at both cutoffs against a bulk correlation gain of +0.031. a blanket claim that correlation is invalid would exceed the evidence. the narrower attribution problem survives though, per-unit correlation and selection gains track at top-10% (ρ = +0.679) but not at top-1% (ρ = +0.168, p = 0.33), so an aggregate correlation gain does not identify which assays improved at a sharp cutoff.

what does the GEM-Bio side add?

a top-20% cutoff that all four corpora support, which is where the PETML result lives: selecting the top 20% of variants on 10 published plastic-degradation studies captures essentially none of the achievable gain, −0.016 for ESM-2 and −0.006 for ESM-C, against +0.326, +0.461, and +0.423 on SSMuLA, Rubisco, and ProteinGym. and the Rubisco controls, which are the cleanest demonstration that a headline correlation is a composite: ESM-2 reaches ρ = +0.631 against maximum turnover across 8,760 single mutants, restricting to well-measured variants drops it to +0.364, and partialling out wild-type residue preference drops it to +0.259. positive signal survives, but the raw number was substantially measurement quality and site conservation.

Limitations. one scientific domain, and units within a corpus are not fully independent because they share experimental conventions. the family offset is estimated from ProteinGym and SSMuLA only; Rubisco and PETML show instability across regimes but are too few or too heterogeneous to estimate comparable offsets. the mechanism is unidentified: dead-variant fraction, library size, score dispersion, and label skew each close at most 39% of the family gap, and mutation regime closes 96% but is almost perfectly confounded with family membership since every SSMuLA unit is multi-mutant. the PETML corpus has 10 usable studies against roughly 120 needed for 80% power on an effect of the observed size, so the near-zero utility is interpretable and the between-corpus comparison is not. the Rubisco controls come from one protein under one protocol.

Takeaways

what the three add up to

the argument none of the three posters makes alone is about ordering. a verifier failure, a representational failure and a metric failure are usually treated as separate literatures with separate remedies, but each was found the same way and each is invisible to the summary its own field reports. that suggests the remedy is not three fixes but one habit: report the quantity the decision consumes alongside the quantity that summarises the distribution, and show a control that could have failed.

the concession belongs here rather than in a footnote. these three share a method and a domain more firmly than they share a finding. one scientific area, one author, one family of techniques, and three levels chosen after the results were in. to claim them as a unified result about evaluation in general would be the same overreach the posters are each about: a summary statistic asked to carry a decision it cannot support. what they do support is narrower and still worth saying, which is that in every place this test was run, the reported number and the decision number came apart, and nothing in the standard reporting would have shown it.

for a reader the practical consequence is small and concrete: when a number is quoted at you, ask what cutoff the decision uses and ask for the number at that cutoff. if the two are the same, nothing here applies. if nobody has computed the second one, that is the finding.

the verifier is attackable
a trust gate certifies a memorizing scorer at AUROC 0.991 and 100% pass rate while the agent loses about 1.05 good candidates per batch, and the defence that fixes it cannot see the next attack.
the representation may not resolve the question
biologically distinct mutational processes reach the same ESM-2 destination, and validation computed in that same representation is not independent evidence.
the reported metric is not the acted-on metric
benchmark correlation explains half the variance in top-10% selection value and 16% at top-1%, and the same correlation means different things in different benchmark families.
report the boundary too
each poster names where its own critique stops: a withdrawn pathology, a scale-dependent collapse rate reported as two numbers, a supervised regime where correlation and selection improve together.

Limitations

Limitations across the line. all three studies are offline and counterfactual on pools that prior experimental choices already selected, so they support claims about differences between policies, representations, and metrics rather than population-level statements about protein space. none involves an LLM-driven agent, so transfer to the systems the framing is aimed at is untested. the domain is protein function throughout, which is what makes ground truth available and also what limits the scope. and the release ledger has GEM-Bio on venue hold, Trust-AI-Eval conditional, AgenticLS as position-only after the matched-compute control, and Verify-Agents conditional on a citation check, so none of these numbers should be quoted outside their papers yet.
