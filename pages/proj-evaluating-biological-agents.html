Abstract

a closed-loop protein-design agent does not measure most of what it decides on. it ranks thousands of candidates with a cheap proxy, trusts that the proxy works because a benchmark reported a good correlation, and delegates ranking to a supplied scorer only if a trust gate clears it. all three are measurements, and all three are read off a static corpus before the agent starts choosing. this project stress-tests each one in a setting where measured ground truth exists. a zero-shot ESM-2 650M score ranks measured PET-hydrolase activity below a plain mutation count. reported correlation explains half the variance in top-10% selection value and 16% at top-1%, the regime a campaign operates in. a trust gate that scores a supplied scorer on outcomes it may have seen certifies a scorer that memorized them at AUROC 0.991. the common failure is that a static number is not the quantity an optimizer pushes on.

the proxy: a score that cannot beat counting

AI for Science @ NeurIPS 2026Poster

a protein fitness proxy is a computable stand-in for a slow wet-lab measurement, and a design campaign ranks thousands of candidates by it and tests the top ones. the simplest bar a proxy has to clear is a trivial baseline, and the clearest trivial baseline for a mutant is how many mutations it carries. on 504 measured PET-hydrolase variants across 25 studies, a zero-shot ESM-2 650M log-likelihood score ranks activity at a within-study Spearman of 0.01, a substitution matrix scores below zero, a hydropathy feature does better, and a plain mutation count reaches 0.25. the expensive model is the weakest of the four scorers on the property a design campaign would use it for.

Finding 1

a zero-shot protein language model ranks measured PETase activity at a within-study Spearman of 0.01, worse than a mutation count at 0.25, so it adds nothing over a trivial baseline on the task it is used for. a proxy that cannot clear counting is not evidence about function, whatever its benchmark correlation elsewhere.

why run the audit before the campaign, not after?

because the proxy is the score people reach for first when they rank designs without measuring them, and the moment a search starts choosing the candidates it likes best, the proxy is steering the campaign rather than describing it. the check belongs before the first batch is synthesized, not after the money is spent.

the benchmark: correlation stops predicting where campaigns act

Trust-AI-Eval @ NeurIPS 2026GEM-Bio @ NeurIPS 2026Poster

a protein benchmark reports the Spearman correlation between model scores and measured outcomes, which summarizes ranking quality over a whole assay. a design campaign does something else: it takes the top few candidates and tests them. those are different quantities, and this measures how much the first predicts the second, across 70 benchmark units in four corpora: 41 ProteinGym scans, 16 SSMuLA combinatorial libraries, three Rubisco kinetic axes from 8,760 single mutants, and 10 PETML plastic-degradation campaigns.

Finding 2

correlation becomes less informative exactly where campaigns act. it explains half the variance in top-10% selection value and 16% at top-1%. three of the 41 ProteinGym assays produce worse-than-random top-1% selections despite positive reported correlations, the worst at selection value −0.300 on a reported ρ of +0.105.

Finding 3

the same reported correlation means different things in different benchmark families. at matched correlation, SSMuLA libraries deliver +0.265 more top-1% selection value than ProteinGym assays (Mann-Whitney p = 0.0001), and +0.147 at top-10% (p = 0.0022). a second scorer family reproduces the offset at +0.252 (p = 0.0003). an aggregate leaderboard score mixes model behavior with benchmark composition, so two methods evaluated on different family mixtures are not comparable through the reported scalar alone.

Finding 4

the obvious label-free fix does not transfer. score-distribution skew looks promising retrospectively, correlating with residual top-1% selection value at +0.633 across 60 units, but its sign reverses across families: fitted on ProteinGym and applied to SSMuLA it gives −0.388, and a fourth corpus reverses it again, −0.783 within PETML against +0.551 within ProteinGym. skew adds information alongside the reported correlation, and fails as a standalone transferable rule.

where is the boundary of the critique?

where a supervised model has genuine signal, correlation and selection improve together: across 12 assays and three cross-validation schemes on identical folds, mean selection gain is +0.035 at both cutoffs against a bulk correlation gain of +0.031. a blanket claim that correlation is invalid would exceed the evidence. the narrower attribution problem survives: an aggregate correlation gain does not identify which assays improved at a sharp cutoff.

the verifier: a trust gate that certifies memorization

Verify-Agents @ NeurIPS 2026Poster

a closed-loop agent ranks a pool of unmeasured candidates with a supplied scorer, tests a batch, and feeds the outcomes back. before trusting the scorer, a gate scores it on the outcomes collected so far. the gate never verifies that the supplied scorer is parameter-free, which is the vulnerability. the attacks below are ordered by the defense each one defeats.

  1. the leakage attacka scorer that memorizes the observed outcomes and outputs noise everywhere else. across 4,920 trials, a fully leaked scorer reaches gate AUROC 0.991 and passes 100% of trials, against 0.663 and 77.2% for a truthful zero-shot scorer. it ranks above the useful scorer and performs at random on the unmeasured pool, costing about 1.05 good candidates per batch.
  2. forward validation, and why it is not enoughscore the scorer only on measurements collected after it was supplied. this removes the leakage advantage: the truthful scorer holds at 0.687 while the fully leaked scorer falls to 0.495, the finite-sample false-pass floor rather than residual signal.
  3. the pool-side attack forward validation cannot seeleave every measured candidate untouched and corrupt only the unmeasured pool. because the measured scores are unchanged, both gates return identical statistics to the truthful scorer by construction, 0.663 and 0.676. the selections lose 1.24 good candidates per batch.
  4. top-pick probing, and probe-aware evasionmeasure a prefix of the scorer's own top picks before committing the batch. a 25% probe detects 99.2% of naive pool-side attacks while costing the truthful scorer 4.4% of its yield. an attacker who baits the top of the ranking to match the probe drops detection to 45.9%.
Finding 5

a trust gate that evaluates a supplied scorer in sample certifies a scorer that has memorized the evaluation outcomes, ranking it above a truthful one at AUROC 0.991 against 0.663. forward validation closes that surface and nothing else: a pool-side attacker produces identical gate statistics by construction. detection is still not recovery, the probe catches 99.2% of naive attacks but recovers only about 10% of the lost value when the fallback is random, since catching an attack and replacing it are different problems.

why does detection without a fallback fail?

because if being caught sends the agent to random selection, and the attacker's goal was already to reduce the agent to random, detection imposes almost no cost on the attacker. a verifier needs a fallback that preserves value, which means an independent source of capability outside the compromised scorer's control. an in-house ridge model trained on the campaign's own 48 measurements recovers 17% at a 25% probe and overtakes the supplied scorer entirely after roughly 115 measurements. verification can tell you when you need that. it cannot supply it.

Takeaways

what the three studies share

counting is the bar
a zero-shot proxy ranks measured activity at 0.01 against 0.25 for a mutation count, so it has to earn its cost against counting first.
the number decays where it is used
reported correlation explains half the variance at top-10% and 16% at top-1%, the regime a campaign operates in.
in-sample gates certify memorization
scoring a supplied scorer on outcomes it may have seen rewards the scorer that memorized them, at AUROC 0.991.
a static number is not the optimized one
in all three, the quantity measured on a fixed corpus stops meaning what it claimed once an optimizer selects on it.

Limitations

each axis is scoped to its own data. the proxy result is about PET-hydrolytic activity and melting temperature for these assays and these scorers, not a universal ranking of protein models, and zero-shot log-likelihood is one readout among several. the benchmark result is one scientific domain, the family offset is estimated from ProteinGym and SSMuLA only, and the between-corpus PETML comparison is underpowered at 10 studies. the verifier evaluation is offline and counterfactual on pools that earlier experimental choices already selected, so differences between policies are interpretable while absolute performance is not a population estimate, and transfer to an LLM-driven research agent is untested.
