CortSeer: an anatomical prior recovers FCD lesions MRI misses

Saanvi S. Subramanian · Caltech SURF, with the LENS lab (Lab for Early-Age Neuroimaging at Stanford)

oral, ECCV AI for Medical 3D Vision workshop (AI4M3D), September 2026

Focal cortical dysplasia is a surgically treatable cause of drug-resistant epilepsy, and many confirmed lesions are missed on conventional MRI. CortSeer asks whether restricting a trained 3D segmentation to an anatomical localization zone, a stand-in for the region an EEG-guided workup would indicate, can recover the lesions an image-only model misses.

Method: zone-restricted recovery over a trained detector

The detector is a 3D nnU-Net trained on FLAIR volumes with five-fold cross-validation. On top of its output, the recovery stage adds no training: SynthSeg parcellation defines anatomical zones, the search is restricted to the suspected zone, and a multi-peak search looks for several candidate peaks there rather than trusting a single maximum. Two null baselines calibrate the recovery: shuffling the detector's probabilities inside each zone, and drawing a random connected cluster of matched size inside the zone.

A localization zone recovers 14 of 25 missed lesions

On 175 confirmed-FCD scans, the baseline detects 150 (85.7%) and misses 25 completely, where a complete miss means no voxel above threshold inside the lesion. Restricting the multi-peak search to the correct lobe recovers 14 of those 25, with every recovery appearing within the top four candidate peaks. The null baselines recover 1.4 (probability shuffle) and 4.0 (random in-zone cluster), so the recovery clears chance at P below 1e-4 against both.

The recovery leans on the zone restriction rather than on raw detector confidence: only 8 of the 14 recovered lesions clear the per-case detection threshold on their own. Of the 25 misses, 24 are frontal or temporal.

The 14 of 25 is an upper bound, not an EEG result

The localization zone is derived from each case's own confirmed lesion lobe, used as a proxy for the region an EEG workup would indicate. No EEG signal or EEG report is processed anywhere in the pipeline, so this recovery is a best-case, idealized-localization ceiling rather than an achievable EEG-guided number. A companion analysis on the fusion side shows that a realistically accurate prior applied after segmentation transfers almost no benefit, with a break-even near chance, so the gap between this ceiling and a deployable result is the open problem, not a solved one.

Limitations

The localization zone is simulated from the confirmed lesion lobe, not from a measured EEG report or raw signal, so every recovery number is a ceiling a measured prior would approach. The recovery stage has no held-out split or external test set yet, and a documented-only, FCD-only retrain of the detector has not been run, since 18 of the confirmed-FCD cases were in the detector's training data. The numbers on this page are the 14-of-25 framing on the 175-case confirmed-FCD cohort; a separate journal manuscript frames the same work on a 231-case all-comers cohort with different denominators, and the two should not be mixed.
