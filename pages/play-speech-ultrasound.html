Speech in ultrasound and in silico

I ran one speech experiment through two systems. The first is a functional ultrasound (fUSI) recording of auditory cortex. The second is Meta's TRIBE v2, a brain foundation model that predicts fMRI responses from audio, text and video. In the ultrasound data, I measured the blood-response timing directly. The original pipeline shifted event onsets by −1.6 s, and that shift went the wrong way. The best fit was +2.8 s, and it held on held-out blocks. The response was also broader than the fMRI model assumes, with a center of mass of 7.3 s against 5.3 s. I then replayed the same block design through TRIBE with acoustic controls and found that its audio pathway treats time-reversed speech as speech. Its text pathway restores the response only when a transcriber can recover the words (93% for vocoded speech). Along the way I found and fixed a transcript-placement bug in TRIBE's pipeline. I also built a tested, reproducible codebase that recovers effects planted in synthetic data.

2, Response shapes

I normalized each response to its own peak over 0–25 s and marked its center of mass, which I computed over 0–40 s. The measured ultrasound response is slower and broader than the fMRI convention. TRIBE's prediction is faster.

3, What does the model treat as speech?

This panel plots TRIBE's block-locked prediction for a control condition next to the matched speech block. I define retention as the control response divided by the speech response. A retention of 1 means the model treats the control like speech, and 0 means the model ignores it. These curves are model predictions. I did not measure them in a brain.
