\section*{The question}

When we evaluate an AI agent doing biology, are we measuring whether it found something true, or only whether
it scored well on the signal we happened to hand it?

\section*{01 --- The challenge}

An agent doing biology is judged by a signal: a verifier's verdict, a benchmark correlation, a validation score
computed in some representation. The agent gets scrutinised and the signal does not. But the signal is where
the assumption lives. In each case below the arithmetic is right and the conclusion is wrong, and the failure
is invisible from the headline number.

Biology is where this is checkable, because deep mutational scans record the outcome for nearly every candidate
in a pool, so an agent's decisions can be replayed and counterfactual actions scored directly.

\paragraph{Complete-outcome domain.} A task where nearly every available action already has a recorded
experimental result. This is the property that makes the verifier itself measurable.

\paragraph{Selection value.} The fraction of achievable gain captured by acting on a model's top-ranked
candidates. An oracle ranking gives 1, random gives 0. This is what a campaign receives and not what a
benchmark reports.

\paragraph{Action-space identifiability.} Whether the candidate actions an agent chooses among reach
distinguishable destinations in its representation.

\section*{03 --- Results}

\subsection*{Thread 1: When the verifier is wrong}

\emph{When the Verifier Is Wrong: Red-Teaming Agent Verification Where Ground Truth Exists.}
NeurIPS Verify-Agents 2026. \todo{OpenReview link once the decision is out.}

A closed-loop protein-design agent picks candidates from a pool of thousands, and a trust gate decides whether
to delegate that ranking to a supplied scorer by checking its AUROC against outcomes collected so far. The gate
never verifies that the supplied scorer is actually parameter-free. 41 deep mutational scans of 1{,}800--7{,}900
candidates each, plus five published multi-round campaigns, from ProteinGym, FLIP and PETML.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
  \item \textbf{Leakage attack.} Across 4{,}920 trials a fully leaked scorer reaches gate AUROC 0.991 and
  passes 100\% of trials, against 0.663 and 77.2\% for an honest zero-shot scorer. It performs at random on
  the unmeasured pool, costing about 1.05 good candidates per batch. Even 25\% leakage passes 77\% of the time.
  \item \textbf{Forward validation.} Scoring the scorer only on measurements collected after it was supplied
  removes the leakage advantage: honest holds at 0.687 and 79.5\%, fully leaked falls to 0.495 and 34.8\%,
  which is the finite-sample false-pass floor rather than residual signal.
  \item \textbf{Pool-side attack.} Leaving every measured candidate untouched and corrupting only the
  unmeasured pool returns gate statistics identical to the honest scorer by construction, 0.663 and 0.676,
  while losing 1.24 good candidates per batch.
  \item \textbf{Top-pick probing.} A 25\% probe detects 99.2\% of naive pool-side attacks at a 4.4\% cost to
  honest yield. A probe-aware attacker that baits the top of the ranking drops detection to 45.9\%, and 39.6\%
  at a 50\% probe.
\end{itemize}

\finding{1}{A trust gate that evaluates a supplied scorer in sample certifies a scorer that has memorised the
evaluation outcomes, ranking it above an honest one at AUROC 0.991 against 0.663.}

\finding{2}{Forward validation closes the leakage surface and nothing else. A pool-side attacker is identical
to the honest scorer from the gate's view by construction.}

\finding{3}{Detection is not recovery. The probe catches nearly every naive attack but recovers about 10\% of
lost value with random fallback, moving the agent from $-0.162$ to $-0.036$ where honest would give $+1.082$.
An in-house ridge model on the campaign's own 48 measurements recovers 17\% at a 25\% probe and 28\% at 50\%,
and overtakes the supplied scorer after roughly 115 measurements, reaching $+1.745$ against $+0.741$ at 384.}

Four further verifier pathologies are measured against ground truth: a sampled stochastic baseline that biases
the comparison toward itself (43.1\% to 56.9\% decision accuracy once the expectation is used); verifier
selection that does not survive held-out tasks (the incumbent moves from second to last across 35 held-out
scans); a protocol mismatch worth about a fifth of the observed gap; and a gate statistic whose resolution
cannot support its threshold (fold enrichment moves in discrete steps --- step size UNRESOLVED, see
% REVISIONS.md: paper says 48/25=1.92 over four values, REVISIONS says six values, REVIEWS computes 2.0 ---
and is exactly zero for 34.3\% of
estimates). A fifth appeared on 13 tasks and disappears across all 41, so it is withdrawn.

\textbf{Limitations.} Offline and counterfactual on pools that prior experimental choices already selected.
Six non-LLM policy classes; damage follows the fraction of action selection delegated to the compromised
scorer, with the gate reading 0.6632 under both conditions and changing its decision in 0 of 1{,}640 trials.
Transfer to an LLM-driven agent is untested. \todo{Resolve the 7 unverified citations flagged in REVISIONS.md.}

\subsection*{Thread 2: Representational identifiability}

\emph{Representational Identifiability Limits Action Selection in a Protein-Design Agent.}
NeurIPS AgenticLS 2026. \todo{OpenReview link. Also decide whether this page or the existing
action-identifiability stub is the canonical home --- the stub says MoML 2026 and this says AgenticLS, and both
cannot be the whole story.}

A hypothesise--simulate--validate loop for mutational-driver discovery over ESM-2 and the 78 COSMIC signatures.
The agent proposes a process, enacts its mean hidden-state displacement by activation steering, and compares
the enacted direction against the real mutation's displacement.

Three biologically distinct processes --- SBS1 (clock-like), SBS7a (UV), SBS2 (APOBEC) --- land at pairwise
cosine distance $\leq 0.011$, while SBS17a sits at 0.04--0.08. SBS2 and SBS7a do have similar COSMIC spectra
(cosine 0.71), but SBS2 and SBS1 are near-orthogonal in COSMIC (cosine 0.02) and still merge in ESM-2.

\finding{4}{Across all 3{,}003 pairs of the 78 signatures, distinct processes often reach similar destinations,
and the separation that exists tracks broad substitution composition rather than trinucleotide context.
Scrambling context while preserving substitution marginals moves the destination by $0.0081 \pm 0.0016$ against
a seed floor of $0.0014 \pm 0.0005$ --- $5.8\times$ above the floor, but 3.6--$7.3\times$ smaller than the
separation between signatures of differing composition.}

\finding{5}{The measurement procedure was doing much of the work. Stop-at-first-stop translation turns 524 of
2{,}028 mutants (25.8\%) into one-residue placeholders, and placeholder count alone explains $R^2 = 0.683$ of
pairwise distance at 8M and 0.870 at 650M. Length control removes the artifact, but the binary collapse rate
becomes scale-dependent: 23.8\% of pairs at or below 0.011 at 8M against 72.5\% at 650M. Both are reported and
neither is claimed as a catalogue-wide rate.}

\finding{6}{The embedding-proximity objective is exploitable and the validation check shares its
representation. Unconstrained single-residue edits drive distance to the PF17041 centroid from 0.26 to 0.009
over 200 edits, 5.0 sd below the natural-protein reference mean of $0.194 \pm 0.037$ across 25 proteins, while ESM-2
naturalness falls from $-0.41$ to $-0.50$, 2.2 sd below the natural band. The removed plausibility signal does
detect the departure, but validation in the same representation the agent optimises is not independent
evidence.}

\todo{Decide whether the capability results --- 26.2\% recommender top-1 against 1.3\% for the strongest
frequency baseline, falling to near chance on held-out targets and processes, SBS17a enacted at cosine
$0.53 \pm 0.03$ --- belong on this page at all, or whether they are context that invites the reader to score
the agent when the point is to score the representation.}

\textbf{Limitations.} Steering and decoding cover a subset of signatures while the destination analysis covers
all 78. ESM-2 8M and 650M share training data and tokenisation, so the scale comparison is not an
independent-model replication. A matched-compute control erases the apparent agency gain: at 100 scorer calls,
fixed and random search reach 0.1586 and 0.1585 final centroid distance against 0.1625 for the adaptive policy,
which is why the release ledger has this poster as position-only. That control does not touch the
identifiability result.

\subsection*{Thread 3: Does benchmark correlation predict selection quality?}

\emph{Does Benchmark Correlation Predict Selection Quality for Protein Design?}
NeurIPS Trust-AI-Eval 2026 and NeurIPS GEM-Bio 2026. \todo{Both OpenReview links. Say on the page how the two
submissions differ rather than letting a reader assume it is one poster twice. As drafted, TAE carries the
top-1\%/top-10\% decay across 60 units and the label-free diagnostic; GEM-Bio carries the top-20\% cutoff all
four corpora support, the PETML near-zero result and the Rubisco controls. GEM-Bio is on venue hold, so the
split may not survive.}

70 units across four corpora: 41 ProteinGym scans, 16 SSMuLA combinatorial libraries, three Rubisco kinetic
axes from 8{,}760 single mutants, 10 PETML plastic-degradation campaigns.

\finding{7}{Correlation becomes less informative exactly where campaigns act. It explains half the variance in
top-10\% selection value ($\rho = +0.707$) and 16\% at top-1\% ($\rho = +0.404$). Three of 41 ProteinGym
assays produce worse-than-random top-1\% selections despite positive reported correlations, the worst at
$-0.300$ on a reported $\rho$ of $+0.105$.}

\finding{8}{The same reported correlation means different things in different benchmark families. At matched
correlation, SSMuLA delivers $+0.265$ more top-1\% selection value than ProteinGym (Mann--Whitney
$p = 0.0001$) and $+0.147$ at top-10\% ($p = 0.0022$). A second scorer family reproduces it at $+0.252$
($p = 0.0003$).}

\finding{9}{The obvious label-free fix does not transfer. Score skew correlates with residual top-1\%
selection value at $+0.633$ across 60 units and $+0.489$ within ProteinGym, but reverses sign across families:
$-0.388$ fitted on ProteinGym applied to SSMuLA, $-0.582$ in reverse, and $-0.783$ within PETML against
$+0.551$ within ProteinGym. It adds information alongside the reported correlation and fails as a standalone
rule.}

\finding{10}{The critique has a boundary. Where a supervised model has real signal, correlation and selection
improve together: across 12 assays and three CV schemes on identical folds, mean selection gain is $+0.035$ at
both cutoffs against a bulk correlation gain of $+0.031$. The narrower attribution problem survives: per-unit
gains track at top-10\% ($\rho = +0.679$) but not at top-1\% ($\rho = +0.168$, $p = 0.33$).}

The GEM-Bio side adds the top-20\% cutoff all four corpora support, where PETML captures essentially none of
the achievable gain ($-0.016$ ESM-2, $-0.006$ ESM-C) against $+0.326$, $+0.461$ and $+0.423$ on SSMuLA,
Rubisco and ProteinGym; and the Rubisco controls, where $\rho = +0.631$ against maximum turnover drops to
$+0.364$ on well-measured variants and $+0.259$ after partialling out wild-type residue preference.

\textbf{Limitations.} One domain; units within a corpus are not fully independent. The family offset is
estimated from ProteinGym and SSMuLA only. The mechanism is unidentified: dead-variant fraction, library size,
score dispersion and label skew each close at most 39\% of the gap, and mutation regime closes 96\% but is
almost perfectly confounded with family since every SSMuLA unit is multi-mutant. PETML has 10 usable studies
against roughly 120 needed for 80\% power at the observed effect size.

None involves an LLM-driven agent, so transfer to the systems the framing targets is untested. The domain is
protein function throughout, which is what makes ground truth available and also what limits the scope. The
release ledger has GEM-Bio on venue hold, Trust-AI-Eval conditional, AgenticLS position-only after the
