About Me

Hello! I'm Saanvi, an undergraduate at Caltech studying Computation and Neural Systems with a minor in Math and Information Science, advised by Markus Meister. I chose CNS because it lets me move between mathematical models and biological experiments.

I want to understand the brain and other living systems well enough to change them deliberately and safely. Interventions in these systems can directly affect health. I want better models to support better treatments, not only better benchmarks.

My current neuroscience research focuses on interpretation and intervention. At Stanford Medicine, I pair pediatric brain MRI with EEG to improve epileptic-lesion detection. In Caltech's Shimojo Lab, I work on studies that use focused ultrasound and brain imaging to investigate noninvasive stimulation.

Beyond neural systems, I use machine learning to study other biological data. In the Perona Lab, I align 3D kidney CT scans with genomic measurements. Earlier, at Seattle Children's, I modeled developmental lineages in medulloblastoma. My protein-language-model work asks whether a model's representation of mutation reflects biology or only the proxy used to evaluate it.

I am especially curious about neuro-based world models: how a brain builds an internal representation of its environment and uses that representation to guide behavior. I want to know what these mechanisms can teach us about artificial systems. This question has led me toward AI alignment. AI's growing ability to reason about biology has also made me interested in biosecurity. These are new interests I am still exploring.

I care about who gets to participate in technical work. I founded and led a SWENext chapter. At Caltech, I now help lead the Neurotechers and Neuro/ML communities.

Outside research, I sing and write. Choir has shaped how I listen to other people, and writing gives me space to work through questions that do not fit inside a research project. Read my essays.
