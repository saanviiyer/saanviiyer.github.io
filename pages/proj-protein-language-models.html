01 · The Challenge

A good score is not the same as a good protein

A protein language model is a model trained on many protein sequences that maps a sequence to a vector or a score. People use those scores to rank candidate proteins and to guide design, keeping the sequences the model likes best. The trouble is that a score can correlate with a real property across ordinary examples and still fail once a search picks the sequences that score highest, because the search can find candidates whose score is high for the wrong reason.

The central question

When a protein model gives a sequence a good score, is that because the sequence is actually good, or because the score is easy to move? And does the answer still hold at the top of the ranking, where a design process would actually use it?

Proxy a computable score used in place of a slow measurement.

Wet-lab measurements are slow, so a model score stands in for binding, stability, or function. That shortcut is useful, and it also means the score, not the property, is what a search actually optimizes against.

Embedding effect versus validated biology a shift in a model's representation is a fact about the model, not yet a fact about the protein.

Much of this work turns on that distinction. An embedding can move in a consistent direction while the move is explained by ordinary sequence properties, so the burden is to show that a residual survives the obvious controls before calling it biology.

02 · The Approach

An audit battery, run before the number is believed

The shared method is an audit battery that stress-tests a score before it is used to rank designs. It asks whether a score beats trivial baselines, whether it stays useful in the top-ranked tail, whether it resists reward hacking, whether it survives transfer to unseen cases, and whether a reported effect survives controls for composition and length. The point is to run the whole battery rather than the one check that happens to pass.

  1. Naturalness metric how protein-like a generated sequence is. A score is only meaningful on sequences that could plausibly be proteins, so the audit measures how far a candidate sits from the distribution of natural proteins and flags candidates that score well only by leaving that distribution.
  2. Leave-signatures-out protocol hold out whole mechanisms, then test transfer. Instead of splitting examples at random, the audit removes entire mechanisms from training and asks the model to handle them at test time, which separates whether information is present from whether it generalizes to something new.
  3. Selection-aware permutation scheme a null that accounts for picking the top candidate. Because a search keeps the best-scoring candidate, the right null is not a random draw but a permutation that mimics the selection, so the baseline reflects how often the effect would appear by chance once you optimize.

Auditing Protein-Function Prediction

No BLAST, New Problems! Auditing Homology-Free Protein-Function Prediction

WiML @ NeurIPS 2026Top 15% of acceptedSole author

PaperCode

This thread builds the audit battery and runs it on one homology-free framework that applies mutational signatures to protein-coding sequences and measures how the mutants move through a protein model's embedding space. The headline observation is real and the controls are the point: a consistent embedding movement is only the start of the evaluation.

Finding 1

SBS17a steers mutants toward PF17041, the SasG_E domain, and the same direction recurs across two Pfam clans, which is what a real effect would produce rather than a single coincidence.

The five audit categories connect directly to that conclusion. The naturalness and baseline checks ask whether the movement needs biology at all, the composition and length controls ask whether it needs the signature specifically, and the transfer check asks whether a model can recognize the mechanism when the surface pattern changes. Run together, they turn the SBS17a to PF17041 observation from a claim about biology into a claim about the model's geometry.

92.5%

Finding 2

After the controls, the residual that is specific to the signature is small, so on this evidence SBS17a is an embedding effect and not a validated functional driver.

Why a naturalness metric?

Because a score can be pushed to extreme values by sequences that are not credible proteins. Without a measure of how protein-like a candidate is, a high score cannot be told apart from a candidate that simply left the natural distribution.

5 SD
how far an unconstrained optimizer pushes the proxy past the natural-protein floor
Finding 3

The fitness proxy looks healthy in bulk, with a Spearman of 0.53, and goes blind where it matters, with top-k utility of 0.15 among the highest-scoring variants, while an unconstrained optimizer reward-hacks it 5 standard deviations past the natural-protein floor.

Finding 4

A probe can recover a signature from an activation at AUC 0.94 while failing to identify unseen signatures at 0.7% top-1, so decodability is not understanding.

Limitations. The audit is demonstrated on one framework and one signature-domain finding. Its generality to other frameworks, signatures, and domains is untested here.

Do Benchmarks Predict Better Designs?

Does Benchmark Correlation Predict Selection Quality for Protein Design?

NeurIPS Trust-AI-Eval 2026NeurIPS GEM Bio 2026Sole author

Paper

A benchmark score is supposed to tell you which scorer will choose better proteins. This thread asks whether a scorer's correlation on a benchmark actually predicts the quality of the designs a search selects with it, and finds that the link is weak exactly where selection happens.

Why this metric?

The result is a rank association between a scorer's benchmark correlation and the value of the designs it selects. It measures whether a higher-correlation scorer tends to select higher-value designs. It is not variance explained on the utility scale, so it does not say how much selection value a correlation buys, only whether the ordering tends to hold.

0.50 → 0.16
squared rank association with selection value, from the top 10% down to the top 1%
Finding 1

The squared rank association with top-10% selection value is 0.50 and falls to 0.16 at the top 1%, so a benchmark correlation predicts selection quality less and less as selection gets more aggressive.

Finding 2

3 of 41 assays give worse-than-random top-1% selections despite positive correlations, and SSMuLA libraries yield 0.265 more selection value than ProteinGym at the same correlation (p = 0.0001). The result replicates on ESM-2 650M and ESM-C 300M.

Identifiability in Protein-Design Agents

Representational Identifiability Limits Action Selection in a Protein-Design Agent

NeurIPS AgenticLS 2026MoML 2026 (MIT)Sole author

Paper

If an agent is going to choose an action from a model's representation, two genuinely different actions must not look the same in that representation. This thread tests, in ESM-2 650M and under this search setup, whether distinct mutational processes stay distinguishable, and finds that much of the apparent distance is an artifact of how sequences are translated.

R² = 0.870
of pairwise destination distance explained by a translation placeholder count, in ESM-2 650M
Finding 1

Across all 3,003 pairs of 78 COSMIC signatures, biologically distinct processes often reach similar destinations. A stop-at-first-stop translation truncates 25.8% of simulated mutants to one-residue placeholders, and the placeholder count explains up to R² = 0.870 of pairwise destination distance, in ESM-2 650M and under this search setup.

Finding 2

The effect is model-dependent: 23.8% placeholder truncation in ESM-2 8M against 72.5% in ESM-2 650M. No broader identifiability conclusion is drawn beyond this model and setup.

Red-Teaming Agent Verifiers

When the Verifier Is Wrong: Red-Teaming Agent Verification Where Ground Truth Exists

NeurIPS Verify-Agents 2026Sole author

Paper

When an agent proposes candidates, a verifier decides which to keep. This thread red-teams that verifier in a setting where ground truth exists, so the real quality of what the verifier keeps can be measured rather than assumed.

Threat model

The leaked-scorer attack assumes the scorer can see the held-out label. The pool-side attack assumes the attacker can shape the candidate pool to match the honest scorer's gate statistics. The defenses assume forward validation on fresh candidates, a sequential probe that spot-checks a fraction of decisions, and an in-house model fallback. Adaptive attackers assume knowledge of the probe.

0.991 AUROC, 0.028 gained
a fully leaked scorer looks near-perfect yet adds almost no good candidates per batch over random
Finding 1

The fully leaked scorer reaches AUROC 0.991 but adds only 0.028 good candidates per batch over random, against 1.082 for the honest scorer, losing 1.05 good candidates per batch. Forward validation prevents the leakage attack.

Finding 2

A pool-side attacker matches the honest scorer's gate statistics to three decimal places while losing 1.24 good candidates per batch. Sequential probing detects 99.2% of attacks at a 25% probe but recovers only 17% of the lost value at that same 25% probe; a 50% probe recovers 28%, and an in-house model fallback also raises recovery to 28%.

Finding 3

Adaptive attackers who know the probe drop its detection from 99.2% to 45.9% at a 25% probe.

Takeaways

What the threads add up to

Controls before conclusions
An embedding effect becomes a biological claim only after composition, length, and transfer controls, and SBS17a does not clear that bar.
Correlation is not selection
A benchmark correlation predicts selection quality less and less as selection sharpens, dropping from 0.50 to 0.16 by the top 1%.
Identifiability has limits
Distinct processes can look alike in a representation, and in ESM-2 650M a translation artifact explains most of the apparent distance.
Verifiers can be gamed
A near-perfect-looking verifier can add almost no real value, and probes detect attacks far better than they recover the lost value.

Limitations across the line. Each result is scoped to the framework, model, and search setup it was measured in. The audits are demonstrated rather than proven general, several threads reuse a small set of assays or signatures, and the appendices marked above still need to be filled before any of these numbers should be quoted outside the papers.
