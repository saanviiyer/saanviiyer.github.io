About Me

Hello! I'm Saanvi, an undergraduate at Caltech studying Computation and Neural Systems with a minor in Math and Information Science, advised by Markus Meister. I chose CNS because it lets me move between mathematical models and biological experiments.

I want to understand the brain and other living systems well enough to change them deliberately and safely, so that better models lead to better treatments and not only better benchmark numbers.

At Stanford Medicine, I pair pediatric brain MRI with EEG in a multimodal foundation model to improve epileptic-lesion detection. The scans sit in separate hospitals that cannot share raw data, which is why I am interested in federated, privacy-preserving learning. In Caltech's Shimojo Lab, I work with focused ultrasound, EEG, and imaging to study noninvasive brain stimulation.

In the Perona Lab, I train a multimodal model that aligns 3D kidney CT scans with genomic measurements. At Seattle Children's I modeled developmental lineages in medulloblastoma. My protein-language-model work asks whether a model's representation of a mutation reflects biology or only the proxy used to score it.

I also build predictive world models for embodied agents. One result, that a faithful heading representation emerges only under partial observability, was accepted for a lightning talk and poster at RoBoWoMo (IEEE IROS 2026).

One question I keep returning to is how a brain builds an internal model of its environment and uses it to act, and what that teaches us about artificial systems. It runs through my work on navigation, continual learning, and protein language models, and it leads into AI safety, whether a model and its safeguards can be trusted the first time they meet something unseen.

I founded and led a SWENext chapter, and at Caltech I help lead the Neurotechers and Neuro/ML communities.

Outside research, I sing in choir and write. Read my essays.
